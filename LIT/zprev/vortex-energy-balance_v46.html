Burgers Vortex Energy Balance

Distilled from the Gemini session on Burgers (1948) and Moffatt, Kida and Ohkitani (1994). Symbols: \(\gamma\) axial stretching rate, \(\Gamma\) circulation, \(\nu\) kinematic viscosity, \(\varepsilon\) imposed dissipation per unit mass. Burgers' strain constant is \(A=\gamma/2\) and his circulation is \(C=\Gamma\).

Checked: the algebra and numbers below were recomputed. Not checked: Gemini's page and equation citations in the two PDFs.

1. One stretched vortex

Velocity profile and core size

With viscous core length \(\delta=\sqrt{\nu/\gamma}\), the azimuthal velocity is

$$U_\theta(R)=\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi R}\left(1-e^{-\gamma R^2/4\nu}\right).$$

Put \(x=\gamma R^2/4\nu\). The velocity peaks where

$$e^{x}-2x-1=0\;\Rightarrow\;x=1.2564,\qquad r_{\max}=\sqrt{4x}\,\sqrt{\frac{\nu}{\gamma}}=2.242\sqrt{\frac{\nu}{\gamma}}.$$ $$U_{\max}=\frac{1-e^{-x}}{2\pi}\,\frac{\Gamma}{r_{\max}}=0.1138\,\frac{\Gamma}{r_{\max}}=0.0508\,\Gamma\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{\nu}},\qquad \Gamma=8.78\,U_{\max}\,r_{\max}.$$

Dissipation of the whole structure

The background strain is irrotational, so integrating the enstrophy isolates the excess caused by the vortex (Burgers eq. 60, Moffatt eq. 3.36):

$$P_{\text{ex}}=\rho\nu\int_0^\infty \omega^2\,2\pi r\,dr=\frac{\rho A C^2}{4\pi}=\frac{\rho\,\gamma\,\Gamma^2}{8\pi}\quad\text{per unit length.}$$

It does not depend on \(\nu\). Moffatt's addition is that non-axisymmetric strain leaves this total unchanged while 98.7% of it falls outside \(r=\sqrt2\,\delta\), where only 37% of the enstrophy sits.

2. Dissipation as flow times pressure drop

The radial inflow through the cylinder \(r=r_{\max}\), per unit length, uses \(\alpha+\beta=-\gamma\):

$$Q=\pi r_{\max}^2\,\gamma .$$

Dividing the dissipation by \(Q\) cancels \(\gamma\):

$$\frac{P_{\text{ex}}}{Q}=\frac{\rho\,\Gamma^2}{8\pi^2 r_{\max}^2}=\frac{\rho\,U_{\max}^2}{2\,(1-e^{-x})^2}=0.977\,\rho\,U_{\max}^2=1.954\cdot\tfrac12\rho U_{\max}^2 .$$

\(P_{\text{ex}}=Q\,\Delta P\) with \(\Delta P\approx\rho U_{\max}^2\), matching the core pressure drop Burgers quotes. The same constant holds for Moffatt's non-axisymmetric strain.

There is no excess axial velocity: the vortex induces only cross-flow motion and \(U_z=\gamma z\) is the background. The "axial energy flux" branch in the session (constants 3.73, 0.244, 0.593, 0.379) was withdrawn by Gemini and should be dropped.

3. Background strain dissipation

For \(U=(\alpha x,\beta y,\gamma z)\) with \(\alpha=-\tfrac12(1+\lambda)\gamma\), \(\beta=-\tfrac12(1-\lambda)\gamma\):

$$\varepsilon_{\text{strain}}=2\nu\left(\alpha^2+\beta^2+\gamma^2\right)=\nu\gamma^2\left(\lambda^2+3\right)\;\xrightarrow{\;\lambda=0\;}\;3\nu\gamma^2 .$$

4. Global budget

\(Q\) is per metre of vortex (m²/s) and \(L_e\) is the vortex length per unit volume (m/m³), so \(L_eQ\) is the volume drawn into the cores per second per unit volume, in 1/s. The budget fixes it:

$$\varepsilon-3\nu\gamma^2=0.977\,(L_eQ)\,U_{\max}^2\quad\Rightarrow\quad L_eQ=\frac{\varepsilon-3\nu\gamma^2}{0.977\,U_{\max}^2}.$$

The core volume fraction is then an output, not an input. Derivation: each metre of tube occupies the cross-section \(\pi r_{\max}^2\) (m³ per m of tube), and there are \(L_e\) metres of tube per m³ of fluid, so the fraction of volume inside the cores is

$$V_f=L_e\,\pi r_{\max}^2 .$$

The inflow per metre of tube is \(Q=\pi\gamma r_{\max}^2\) (section 2), so \(\pi r_{\max}^2=Q/\gamma\) and

$$V_f=\frac{L_eQ}{\gamma}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad L_eQ=V_f\,\gamma .$$

Meaning: the cores hold a fraction \(V_f\) of the volume, and the strain field refills that volume at the rate \(\gamma\), so the volume drawn in per second per unit volume is \(V_f\gamma\). Because \(V_f\) is a fraction of the cylinder's own cross-section, it is a cross-section of the core, not of the whole dissipative eddy. The dissipation spreads over a larger radius than \(r_{\max}\) (98.7% of it lies outside \(r=\sqrt2\,\delta\)); that is a limit of this \(Q\,\Delta P\) bookkeeping.

Correction to the session. Gemini assumed \(V_f=1\%\) as an input and reduced \(L_eQ\) to \(0.0503\,\nu\). That value has units m²/s, because it cancelled \(\pi r_{\max}^2\) against a \(Q\) already evaluated as \(5.03\,\nu\) (which also dropped a factor \(\pi\)). It then discarded the mismatched term as "negligible". The 22.8% eddy share it reported came from that slip. With the units kept, \(L_eQ=V_f\gamma\) and everything is consistent.

5. Closing with Kolmogorov scales

$$\eta=\left(\frac{\nu^3}{\varepsilon}\right)^{1/4},\quad U_\eta=(\nu\varepsilon)^{1/4},\quad \gamma_\eta=\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon}{\nu}},\quad U_\eta\,\eta=\nu .$$

Assumption: the tube's peak-swirl radius and peak velocity scale with the Kolmogorov scales through one dial \(\beta\),

$$r_{\max}=\beta\,\eta,\qquad U_{\max}=\beta\,U_\eta .$$

Fixing the two separately would overdetermine the problem, which caused the repeated loops in the session. Here \(\beta\) is just \(r_{\max}/\eta\), the tube radius in Kolmogorov lengths (the core diameter is \(2\beta\eta\)).

Step 1: what \(x\) is and where it comes from

\(x\) is the dimensionless radius squared of the Burgers vortex,

$$x(R)=\frac{\gamma R^2}{4\nu}=\left(\frac{R}{2\delta}\right)^2,\qquad \delta=\sqrt{\nu/\gamma}.$$

Substituting \(R^2=4\nu x/\gamma\) into \(U_\theta=\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi R}\left(1-e^{-x}\right)\) gives the velocity as a function of \(x\) alone:

$$U_\theta=\frac{\Gamma}{4\pi}\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\;\frac{1-e^{-x}}{\sqrt{x}} .$$

The peak is where \(\mathrm d/\mathrm dx\left[(1-e^{-x})\,x^{-1/2}\right]=0\):

$$e^{-x}x^{-1/2}-\tfrac12(1-e^{-x})x^{-3/2}=0\;\Rightarrow\;2x\,e^{-x}=1-e^{-x}\;\Rightarrow\;e^{x}-2x-1=0 .$$

This has the single positive root (found by Newton iteration) $$\boxed{\,x=1.25643\,},\qquad e^{x}=3.51286=2x+1,\qquad 1-e^{-x}=0.71533 .$$ So \(x\) is a pure number fixed by the shape of the profile. It converts between the peak radius and the viscous scale:

$$r_{\max}^2=\frac{4x\,\nu}{\gamma}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\gamma=\frac{4x\,\nu}{r_{\max}^2}.$$

Step 2: strain rate from \(r_{\max}=\beta\eta\)

The Kolmogorov scales satisfy \(\eta^2=\nu/\gamma_\eta\) with \(\gamma_\eta=\sqrt{\varepsilon/\nu}\). Then \(r_{\max}^2=\beta^2\nu/\gamma_\eta\), and

$$\gamma=\frac{4x\,\nu}{\beta^2\nu/\gamma_\eta}=\frac{4x}{\beta^2}\,\gamma_\eta .$$

Step 3: circulation from \(U_{\max}=\beta U_\eta\)

The peak velocity in terms of \(\Gamma\) and \(r_{\max}\) is \(U_{\max}=\frac{(1-e^{-x})\,\Gamma}{2\pi r_{\max}}\). Solving for \(\Gamma\) and inserting both conditions together with \(U_\eta\eta=\nu\):

$$\Gamma=\frac{2\pi\,r_{\max}U_{\max}}{1-e^{-x}}=\frac{2\pi\,(\beta\eta)(\beta U_\eta)}{1-e^{-x}}=\frac{2\pi}{1-e^{-x}}\,\beta^2\,\nu=8.78\,\beta^2\,\nu .$$

Step 4: the Reynolds numbers

Three Reynolds numbers appear. Each is a velocity times a length over \(\nu\).

\(R_\Gamma\) is the inverse of Moffatt's small parameter, \(\epsilon=\nu/\Gamma=1/R_\Gamma\), so the asymptotic theory needs \(R_\Gamma\gg1\). The core Reynolds number follows from the \(U_{\max}\) relation above:

$$Re_c=\frac{U_{\max}r_{\max}}{\nu}=\frac{\beta U_\eta\cdot\beta\eta}{\nu}=\beta^2,\qquad R_\Gamma=\frac{2\pi}{1-e^{-x}}\,Re_c=8.78\,Re_c=8.78\,\beta^2 .$$

The strain Reynolds number is just Step 1 rearranged, so it is the same for every Burgers vortex: \(Re_\gamma=\gamma r_{\max}^2/\nu=4x\). For comparison, a structure at exactly the Kolmogorov scale has Reynolds number \(U_\eta\eta/\nu=1\), which \(Re_c=\beta^2\) reaches at \(\beta=1\).

Summary:

$$\gamma=\frac{4x\,\nu}{r_{\max}^2}=\frac{4x}{\beta^2}\,\gamma_\eta,\qquad \Gamma=\frac{2\pi\,r_{\max}U_{\max}}{1-e^{-x}}=8.78\,\beta^2\,\nu,\qquad R_\Gamma=\frac{\Gamma}{\nu}=8.78\,\beta^2 .$$

Step 5: strain share of the budget

Start from the background dissipation of section 3, \(\varepsilon_{\text{strain}}=3\nu\gamma^2\), and substitute \(\gamma\) from Step 2:

$$\varepsilon_{\text{strain}}=3\nu\left(\frac{4x}{\beta^2}\gamma_\eta\right)^2=\frac{48\,x^2}{\beta^4}\;\nu\gamma_\eta^2 .$$

Now \(\nu\gamma_\eta^2=\nu\cdot\varepsilon/\nu=\varepsilon\), so

$$\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{strain}}}{\varepsilon}=\frac{48\,x^2}{\beta^4}=\frac{75.8}{\beta^4}\qquad(48x^2=75.77).$$

Step 6: vortex share of the budget

Start from the vortex term of the section 4 budget, \(\varepsilon_{\text{vortex}}=0.977\,(L_eQ)\,U_{\max}^2\), where \(0.977=1/\left(2(1-e^{-x})^2\right)\) comes from section 2. Substitute in turn \(L_eQ=V_f\gamma\) (section 4), then \(\gamma=\frac{4x}{\beta^2}\gamma_\eta\) (Step 2) and \(U_{\max}^2=\beta^2U_\eta^2\):

$$\varepsilon_{\text{vortex}}=0.977\,V_f\,\gamma\,U_{\max}^2=0.977\,V_f\,\frac{4x}{\beta^2}\gamma_\eta\;\beta^2U_\eta^2=0.977\cdot4x\;V_f\;\gamma_\eta U_\eta^2 .$$

The factors \(\beta^2\) cancel, which is what the choice \(U_{\max}=\beta U_\eta\) buys. Next \(\gamma_\eta U_\eta^2=\sqrt{\varepsilon/\nu}\,\sqrt{\nu\varepsilon}=\varepsilon\), so

$$\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{vortex}}}{\varepsilon}=0.977\cdot4x\;V_f=4.91\,V_f .$$

Step 7: closing the budget and the volume fraction

The imposed dissipation is split between the background strain and the vortices, \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{\text{strain}}+\varepsilon_{\text{vortex}}\). Dividing by \(\varepsilon\) and inserting Steps 5 and 6:

$$1=\frac{75.8}{\beta^4}+4.91\,V_f .$$

Solving for the volume fraction gives \(V_f\) as a function of the one dial \(\beta\):

$$\boxed{\;V_f=\frac{1-75.8/\beta^{4}}{4.91},\qquad r_{\max}=\beta\eta\;}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \beta=\left(\frac{75.8}{1-4.91\,V_f}\right)^{1/4}.$$

\(V_f\) must be positive, which needs \(\beta>75.8^{1/4}=2.95\), and cannot exceed \(1/4.91=20\%\) (the vortices would then carry all of \(\varepsilon\)).

Two more quantities complete the table. The inflow per metre of tube is the same for every \(\beta\):

$$Q=\pi\gamma r_{\max}^2=\pi\cdot4x\,\nu=15.8\,\nu ,$$

since \(\gamma r_{\max}^2=4x\nu\) (Step 1). The strain rate (small \(\gamma\)) and circulation (big \(\Gamma\)) follow from the summary of Step 4. Vortex length and spacing

The tube length per unit volume follows from \(L_e=V_f\gamma/Q\). At 1%: \(L_e=0.01\cdot178/1.58\times10^{-5}=1.1\times10^{5}\) m/m³, about 110 m of vortex per litre.

\(L_e\) has units m/m³ = 1/m², so each tube has on average \(1/L_e\) m² of cross-section to itself. For tubes roughly parallel and evenly spread, the mean spacing between axes is

$$d=\sqrt{\frac{V}{L}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L_e}}\quad(L=L_eV\text{ metres of tube in a volume }V),$$

and with \(L_e=V_f/(\pi r_{\max}^2)\) from section 4:

$$d=r_{\max}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{V_f}}=\beta\,\eta\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{V_f}} .$$

So \(d/\eta\) depends on \(\beta\) and \(V_f\) only, not on \(\varepsilon\) or \(\nu\). For random orientation the spacing is of the same order; the exact factor depends on the orientation distribution.

6. A second closure: circulation set by the tube spacing

The budget leaves \(\beta\) free. A second relation comes from taking the circulation as the velocity difference across the mean spacing \(d\) (section 5) times that spacing, with the inertial-range velocity \(U(d)=(\varepsilon d)^{1/3}\):

$$\Gamma=c\,U(d)\,d=c\,\varepsilon^{1/3}d^{4/3}\;\Rightarrow\;\frac{\Gamma}{\nu}=c\left(\frac{d}{\eta}\right)^{4/3},$$

using \(\varepsilon^{1/3}\eta^{4/3}=\nu\). Insert \(\Gamma/\nu=8.78\,\beta^2\) (Step 4) and \(d/\eta=\beta\sqrt{\pi/V_f}\) (spacing section):

$$8.78\,\beta^2=c\,\beta^{4/3}\left(\frac{\pi}{V_f}\right)^{2/3}\;\Rightarrow\;\beta=\left(\frac{c}{8.78}\right)^{3/2}\frac{\pi}{V_f}.$$

Together with the budget \(1=75.8/\beta^4+4.91\,V_f\) this has one root \(V_f\) for each \(c\).

Letting \(c\) depend on \(\varepsilon\)

\(c\) is dimensionless, so it can depend on \(\varepsilon\) only through a Reynolds number; at fixed large scale \(L\) and \(\nu\) that number is \(\propto\varepsilon^{1/3}\). Try

$$c=c_0\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon_0}\right)^{1/3},\qquad c_0=0.71\text{ at }\varepsilon_0=0.1\ \text{W/kg}.$$

The reference \(c_0=0.71\) is an assumed value, not fitted to data; it gives a vortex share of about 12% at 0.1 W/kg, in the range Moffatt quotes. Results for \(\nu=10^{-6}\) m²/s:

7. The data: whole tubes and intense tubes

The literature values of tube radius, swirl and circulation do not describe one population. Papers select tubes in different ways, and the selection changes the swirl and the circulation strongly and the radius only a little. Two sets are therefore distinguished, and every value in the appendix is assigned to one of them.

What is meant by the two sets

  • Intense tubes are the strongest structures only. They are the 1% of the volume with the highest vorticity (the intense vorticity structures or worms of Jiménez et al., Ghira et al., Da Silva et al.; 1.4% in Aligolzadeh et al.), so in the simulations and the 3D measurement they occupy a volume fraction \(V_i\approx0.01\). In the hot-wire records (Mouri et al. 2003, 2004, 2007) the cut is on the signal: the highest 0.1% or 1% of the absolute velocity increments, a share of the samples along the record and not of the volume. The volume that these tubes occupy is not given, so \(V_i\approx0.01\) applies to the DNS-defined tubes only.
  • Whole tubes are tube-like vortices selected with a low threshold or none: the low-pressure cores of Kida et al. (31–35% of the volume), the vortices fitted by Mouri and Hori (2009), the coherent fine-scale eddies of Tanahashi et al., the cores of Pirozzoli and the elementary vortices of Sassa and Makita. They occupy a volume fraction \(V_w\approx0.2\)–0.35, the order of the \(V_f\) of the budget in sections 4 and 5.

The intense tubes are the tail of the distribution of tube strengths and the whole set contains them. The two sets overlap in radius and differ in swirl. The ranges of the values used (rows with an \(Re_\lambda\); the number of values in brackets) are:

How the two sets scale

For intense tubes the swirl follows the r.m.s. velocity: \(V_0/\langle v^2\rangle^{1/2}\approx0.5\)–0.8 (Mouri et al.), \(u_0/u'\approx0.7\)–0.8 (Da Silva et al.) and \(U_{ivs}/u'\approx1.0\)–1.25 (Ghira et al.), so, with \(u'/U_\eta=(Re_\lambda/\sqrt{15})^{1/2}\) and a nearly constant radius \(\beta_i\approx5\),

$$U_i=\kappa\,u'=\kappa\left(\frac{Re_\lambda}{\sqrt{15}}\right)^{1/2}U_\eta,\qquad Re_{c,i}=\beta_iU_i/U_\eta\propto Re_\lambda^{1/2},\qquad \frac{\Gamma_i}{\nu}=8.78\,Re_{c,i}.$$

This agrees with the measured \(Re_0\propto Re_\lambda^{1/2}\) of Mouri et al. For whole tubes the swirl is a few Kolmogorov velocities and grows slowly (\(Re_0\propto Re_\lambda^{0.18}\) of Mouri and Hori, \(\Gamma/\nu\propto Re_\lambda^{0.16}\) of Kida et al., \(W_{\max}/u_K\propto Re_\lambda^{0.28}\) of Sassa and Makita):

$$U_w=c\,\beta_w^{\,q}\,Re_\lambda^{\,s}\,U_\eta,\qquad Re_{c,w}=\beta_wU_w/U_\eta,\qquad \frac{\Gamma_w}{\nu}=8.78\,Re_{c,w},$$

which is the closure of section 9 (with \(c=1\)). The ratio of the two swirls, \(U_i/U_w\propto Re_\lambda^{1/2-s}\), is about 2 at \(Re_\lambda=300\) and about 2.5 at 2000: the intense tubes become relatively stronger as \(Re_\lambda\) rises.

How this enters the model

The dissipation budget of sections 4 and 5 describes the whole set: the volume \(V_f\) it closes is the volume of the tubes that carry the vortex dissipation, and the swirl in it must be that of the whole set,

$$\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{vortex}}}{\varepsilon}=4.91\,V_w\,\frac{(U_w/U_\eta)^2}{\beta_w^{2}}\;\Rightarrow\;V_w=\frac{\left(1-75.8/\beta_w^{4}\right)\beta_w^{2}}{4.91\,(U_w/U_\eta)^{2}}.$$

The intense tubes are a small subset, with a volume \(V_i\approx0.01\) and a share of the dissipation

$$\frac{\varepsilon_i}{\varepsilon}=4.91\,V_i\,\frac{(U_i/U_\eta)^2}{\beta_i^{2}}.$$

With \(V_i=0.01\), \(\beta_i=5\) and \(U_i/U_\eta=9\) (\(Re_\lambda\approx300\)) this is \(\varepsilon_i/\varepsilon\approx0.16\), close to the 15% of the enstrophy that Jiménez et al. find in their intense 1%. So the two sets are consistent with each other in the budget, but they must not be fitted by the same swirl law.

The fits of section 9 mix the two sets: the radii come from intense tubes (Ghira, Jiménez, Aligolzadeh) and one whole-tube radius (Tanahashi), the circulation from intense tubes (Ghira), and the tube Reynolds number from whole tubes (Mouri and Hori 2009). One swirl law then has to serve both, which shows up as the factor 1.5–2.5 between the swirl listed by Ghira et al. and the model swirl, as the 2–3 times too low \(Re_c\) against the intense tubes of Mouri et al., and as the degenerate exponents \(q\) and \(s\). The next step is to fit the two sets separately: the intense set with \(\beta_i\) and \(\kappa\) (or a free exponent), the whole set with the closure of section 9, and to use the whole set in the budget.

The data

The table has one row per source, run and \(Re_\lambda\), sorted by \(Re_\lambda\) and then by source, and gives the tube as the model sees it: the Kolmogorov length \(\eta\) and velocity \(u_\eta\), the radius \(r_{\max}\) and swirl \(U_{\max}\) in SI units and in Kolmogorov units, the tube Reynolds number, the strain \(\gamma\), the circulation \(\Gamma\), the spacing \(d/\eta\) and the tube set (intense or whole, section 7). A dash means that the source does not give the quantity and the Burgers relations cannot supply it. The measured quantities are those of the appendix (A0 and the source sections); the others are completed with the Burgers relations: \(Re_c=r_{\max}U_{\max}/\nu=(r_{\max}/\eta)(U_{\max}/U_\eta)\) when the source gives both, otherwise the Re_c of the source; \(\Gamma/\nu=8.78\,Re_c\) unless the source lists \(\Gamma\); and \(\gamma=4x\gamma_\eta/(r_{\max}/\eta)^2\) from the radius, with \(\gamma_\eta=(\varepsilon/\nu)^{1/2}\).

Step 2: Reynolds numbers of the eddy generator

Take \(L\) as the length scale of the element that generates the eddies and \(U\) as the speed there: for a feed pipe \(L=d_{\text{pipe}}\) and \(U=U_{\text{feed}}\); for an impeller \(L=D\) or the blade width \(W\) (depending on the blade angle) and \(U\) the blade-edge speed, for example \(\pi ND\). Define the process Reynolds number

$$Re=\frac{U\,L}{\nu}.$$

The local dissipation at the generator is \(\varepsilon=U^3/L\) (the same power balance as \(N_pN^3D^5/V\) when \(V\) is the dissipation zone around the impeller, \(V_z=N_pD^3/\pi^3\)). The Taylor-scale Reynolds number of that local turbulence follows from \(Re_\lambda=U'\lambda/\nu\), the isotropic relation \(\varepsilon=15\,\nu U'^2/\lambda^2\), and \(U'=U\):

$$\lambda^2=\frac{15\,\nu\,U^2}{U^3/L}=\frac{15\,\nu L}{U}\;\Rightarrow\;Re_\lambda=\frac{U}{\nu}\sqrt{\frac{15\,\nu L}{U}}=\sqrt{15}\,Re^{1/2}.$$

The Kolmogorov scale is \(\eta=(\nu^3L/U^3)^{1/4}\), so \(L/\eta=Re^{3/4}\). The relation \(Re_\lambda=\sqrt{15}\,(U'/U_\eta)^2\) checks against the data: Makihara et al. quote an rms velocity of \(4.67\,U_K\) at \(Re_\lambda=85\) (\(\sqrt{15}\times4.67^2=84.5\)), and Mouri and Hori's run B1 gives 330 against the listed 332. The assumption \(U'=U\) is an upper bound; inside a jet or impeller zone the rms fluctuation is a fraction of \(U\), which lowers \(Re_\lambda\).

Step 3: core radius, peak swirl and the circulation they imply

Write the core radius and peak swirl in Kolmogorov units,

$$r_{\max}=\beta\,\eta,\qquad U_{\max}=\beta^{\,q}\,U_\eta .$$

In section 9 the swirl also carries a factor \(Re_\lambda^{\,s}\), \(U_{\max}=\beta^{\,q}Re_\lambda^{\,s}U_\eta\) (here \(s=0\)); which tubes the swirl refers to is explained in section 7.

Here \(r_{\max}=2.24\sqrt{\nu/\gamma}\) is the radius of maximum swirl of the Burgers vortex (section 1), the same quantity as \(R_0\) in Mouri and Hori and, up to the factor \(r_{\max}=1.12\,R_B\), the Burgers radius \(R_B=2\sqrt{\nu/\gamma}\) used by Ghira et al. The tube Reynolds number and circulation follow from the Burgers peak velocity \(U_{\max}=(1-e^{-x})\Gamma/(2\pi r_{\max})\), with \(x=1.2564\), \(1-e^{-x}=0.7153\) (section 5) and \(U_\eta\eta=\nu\):

$$Re_c=\frac{U_{\max}r_{\max}}{\nu}=\beta^{\,1+q},\qquad \Gamma=\frac{2\pi\,r_{\max}U_{\max}}{1-e^{-x}}=8.78\,\beta^{\,1+q}\nu\;\Rightarrow\;R_\Gamma=\frac{\Gamma}{\nu}=8.78\,\beta^{\,1+q},\qquad \beta=\left(\frac{R_\Gamma}{8.78}\right)^{1/(1+q)} .$$

\(Re_c\) is the same quantity as \(Re_0=R_0U_0/\nu\) of Mouri and Hori. Two populations appear in the literature and the model follows the first: intense vorticity structures (the 1% of the volume with the highest vorticity), whose swirl scales with the rms velocity \(U'\), and all tubes, whose swirl scales with \(U_\eta\). The radius of the intense structures (\(r_{\max}\approx4.5\)–5 \(\eta\)) is smaller than the radius of all tubes measured with other definitions (\(r_{\max}\approx8\,\eta\) by Mouri and Hori, and by Kida et al. if their core boundary is taken as a Burgers vortex).

Step 4: circulation from the Reynolds number

The circulation Reynolds number rises weakly with \(Re_\lambda\): Mouri and Hori's \(Re_0\) rises as \(Re_\lambda^{0.18}\), Kida et al.'s \(\Gamma/\nu\) as \(Re_\lambda^{0.16}\), and the intense-structure circulation of Ghira et al. as \(Re_\lambda^{0.28}\). Take

$$R_\Gamma=a\,Re_\lambda^{\,p},\qquad p=0.145\;(\text{fitted, section 9; equivalent to }Re^{0.07}\text{ for the process }Re).$$

Then \(\beta\) follows from Step 3 and \(\gamma=4x\gamma_\eta/\beta^2\).

Step 5: dissipation budget and volume fraction

The two sinks are the background strain and the vortices (the swirling flow in the shear layer around each core). From sections 3 and 5 the strain share is \(\varepsilon_{\text{strain}}/\varepsilon=48x^2/\beta^4=75.8/\beta^4\). The vortex term is \(0.977\,L_eQ\,U_{\max}^2\) with \(L_eQ=V_f\gamma\) (sections 2 and 4). Substituting \(\gamma=4x\gamma_\eta/\beta^2\), \(U_{\max}^2=\beta^{2q}U_\eta^2\) and \(\gamma_\eta U_\eta^2=\varepsilon\):

$$\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{vortex}}}{\varepsilon}=0.977\cdot4x\,V_f\,\beta^{\,2q-2}=4.91\,V_f\,\beta^{\,2q-2}.$$

Closing the budget \(1=75.8/\beta^4+4.91\,V_f\beta^{2q-2}\) gives

$$\boxed{\;V_f=\frac{\left(1-75.8/\beta^{4}\right)\beta^{\,2-2q}}{4.91},\qquad \text{vortex share}=1-\frac{75.8}{\beta^4},\qquad \frac{d}{\eta}=\beta\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{V_f}}\;}$$

with \(L_e=V_f/(\pi r_{\max}^2)=1/d^2\) (section 5). The volume fraction is that of the cylinders of radius \(r_{\max}\).

9. Four-parameter closure: swirl growing with \(Re_\lambda\)

Section 8 writes the swirl of the tube in Kolmogorov units, \(U_{\max}=\beta^{\,q}U_\eta\), so that it depends on the Reynolds number only through \(\beta\). The measured swirl grows with \(Re_\lambda\) at a nearly constant radius (the radius falls slowly, 4.28 to 3.90 \(\eta\) in Ghira et al. 2022, while the circulation rises), so this section adds one exponent to the swirl law, \(U_{\max}=\beta^{\,q}Re_\lambda^{\,s}U_\eta\), and fits the closure to the source data of section 7 and the appendix. The fit uses rows of both sets of section 7 (whole and intense tubes); it is to be split into one fit per set.

The closure

The total circulation keeps its growth law, and the swirl gets a Reynolds-number factor:

$$R_\Gamma=\frac{\Gamma}{\nu}=a\,Re_\lambda^{\,p},\qquad \frac{U_{\max}}{U_\eta}=\beta^{\,q}\,Re_\lambda^{\,s}.$$

For a Burgers vortex \(R_\Gamma=8.78\,\beta\,U_{\max}/U_\eta\) (section 8, Step 3), so \(\beta\) follows from the two laws:

$$\beta^{\,1+q}=\frac{a\,Re_\lambda^{\,p-s}}{8.78},\qquad Re_c=\beta\,\frac{U_{\max}}{U_\eta}=\frac{R_\Gamma}{8.78}.$$

Two consequences are worth noting. The tube Reynolds number \(Re_c\) is fixed by the circulation alone, so the Mouri and Hori values and the Ghira et al. circulations constrain the same function \(R_\Gamma(Re_\lambda)\), that is \(a\) and \(p\). The radius data then fix \(q\) and \(s\) through \(\beta\). The dissipation budget of section 8, Step 5, is unchanged, but with the swirl written \(U=U_{\max}/U_\eta\) the volume fraction is $$V_f=\frac{\left(1-75.8/\beta^{4}\right)\beta^{2}}{4.91\,U^{2}},\qquad \text{vortex share}=1-\frac{75.8}{\beta^4},\qquad \frac{d}{\eta}=\beta\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{V_f}},$$

which reduces to Step 5 of section 8 when \(s=0\). A swirl that grows with \(Re_\lambda\) therefore lowers \(V_f\): for a given share of the dissipation fewer tubes are needed when each carries more.

Fit

The four constants were found by minimising the sum of squared normalised residuals \(\chi^2\) was minimised with a global minimiser and \(\sigma_{\text{ref}}\) was iterated with the fit (footnote † of the table). The Ghira et al. radius group is then fitted almost exactly and its \(\sigma_{\text{ref}}\) collapses (0.4% in a trial run), so a floor of 5% was applied to \(\sigma_{\text{ref}}\); the circulation and the tube Reynolds number, and so \(a\) and \(p\), are the same with or without the floor. Three searches from different seeds agree.

$$\boxed{\;a=76.8\pm8.7,\quad p=0.145\pm0.017,\quad q=0.30\pm0.10,\quad s=0.18\pm0.03\;}\qquad\left(R_\Gamma=76.8\,Re_\lambda^{0.145},\quad \frac{U_{\max}}{U_\eta}=\beta^{0.30}Re_\lambda^{0.18}\right)$$

The mean of \(\sigma_{\text{norm}}^2\) is \(\chi^2/N=1.05\) (\(N=33\)). The parameters are correlated, \(a\)–\(p\) \(-0.99\) and in particular \(q\)–\(s\) \(-0.98\): the radius data fix the combination of \(q\) and \(s\) that sets the radius trend, not each separately. A trial run without the floor gave \(q=0.12\), \(s=0.23\) with the same \(a\) and \(p\) and the same model values to within a few per cent, so \(q\) and \(s\) should not be quoted on their own.

Limits of this model

  • Dissipation share. The vortex share is a result of the budget, \(1-75.8/\beta^4\), and rests on one uniform strain \(\gamma\) for the whole volume outside the tubes. The measurements give 14–75% depending on threshold and definition (core only, intense subset, wavelet-coherent part). Tube plus surrounding shear layer has not been measured as one quantity.
  • Two sets of tubes. The measured swirl and tube Reynolds number differ by a factor 2–3 between whole and intense tubes (section 7), and one tube with one swirl law cannot describe both. The all-tube radius of Mouri and Hori (8–9 \(\eta\)) is biased high by their own statement and is larger than every other radius in the data. The experimental radius of Aligolzadeh et al. (6.0 \(\eta\) at \(Re_\lambda=179\), objectively defined vortex boundary) is the largest residual of the fit.
  • Circulation definition. The circulation of Ghira et al. is listed as \(\Gamma_{ivs}=2\pi\langle U_{ivs}\rangle R_{ivs}\), the circulation inside \(R_{ivs}\) for a Burgers vortex, and is taken here as the total circulation of the model. With that reading the model agrees in magnitude with Kida et al. and Mouri and Hori, but the swirl listed by Ghira et al. (\(U_{ivs}/u_\eta=6.0\)–10.2) is 1.5–2.5 times the model's \(U_{\max}/U_\eta\), so the model describes the circulation of the intense tubes and not their swirl (see the appendix).
  • Volume fraction. \(V_f\) is the volume within \(r_{\max}\) and is not the quantity reported as a threshold volume (1% for intense structures, 0.8–20% at Hosokawa and Yamamoto's thresholds) or as the core volume (31–35%, Kida et al.).
  • Isolated straight Burgers tubes. Measured tubes are about \(55\,\eta\) long (Ghira et al.), curved, and wrapped by spiral vortex layers (Kida et al., Makihara et al.). Axial outflow and end effects are not modelled.
  • Extrapolation. The fit covers \(Re_\lambda=36\)–1934. The feed-pipe case (274) lies inside that range; the impeller case (2170) lies just above the highest laboratory value and about twice the highest DNS in the sources. Jiménez and Wray note that the intense-structure trends cannot continue indefinitely.
  • Process mapping. \(\varepsilon=U^3/L\) is a scale estimate good to a factor of a few, and \(U'=U\) is an upper bound. The vessel-average \(\varepsilon\) is lower (2.4 W/kg for the impeller example) and corresponds to larger tubes.

Appendix: the source data, how it was converted, and what the model uses

Every literature value that enters or is compared with the model is listed here, source by source. For each paper there is a short discussion of how the tubes were selected and the equations that convert the tabulated numbers to the model quantities, followed by one table per measurement type, with the rows in ascending \(Re_\lambda\). Each table has the measurement type, \(Re_\lambda\), the kinematic viscosity \(\nu\) and the dissipation rate \(\varepsilon\) of the run (the two given quantities that fix \(\eta\) and \(u_\eta\); in the units of the paper, which differ: DNS code units, cm\(^2\)/s and m\(^2\)/s\(^3\); a dash where the paper does not give one), then the entries taken from the paper's table, then the value used in the model with the \(\nu\), \(\varepsilon\) and \(\eta\) used by the model in SI units, and the role: fitted in section 9, or not fitted with the reason. The model-side \(\nu\) and \(\varepsilon\) are measured for the laboratory experiments in air (Mouri et al.), derived from the Kolmogorov length for Aligolzadeh et al. (‡), and for all DNS rows a reference, water (\(\nu=10^{-6}\) m\(^2\)/s) at \(\varepsilon=0.1\) W/kg (†), explained in A0b. The tube set of each source (whole or intense) is explained in section 7.

A0. Conversions used for all sources

A Burgers vortex has \(u_\theta(r)=\dfrac{\Gamma}{2\pi r}\left(1-e^{-r^2/R_B^2}\right)\) with \(R_B=2\sqrt{\nu/\gamma}\), the radius at which the vorticity has fallen to \(1/e\) of its axis value. The swirl is maximal at \(r_{\max}=\sqrt{x}\,R_B=1.121\,R_B\), where \(x=1.2564\) is the root of \(e^x-2x-1=0\), and

$$U_{\max}=\frac{(1-e^{-x})\,\Gamma}{2\pi\,r_{\max}},\qquad \frac{\Gamma}{\nu}=\frac{2\pi}{1-e^{-x}}\,\frac{r_{\max}U_{\max}}{\nu}=8.78\,Re_c,\qquad Re_c=\frac{r_{\max}U_{\max}}{\nu}.$$

The circulation inside a radius \(r\) is \(\Gamma(r)=\Gamma\left(1-e^{-r^2/R_B^2}\right)\) and the swirl at \(r=k\,r_{\max}\) is \(U_{\max}f(k)\), with

$$f(k)=\frac{1-e^{-xk^2}}{k\,(1-e^{-x})}.$$

The Kolmogorov scales satisfy \(\nu=u_\eta\,\eta\), and for isotropic turbulence the rms velocity is \(u'/u_\eta=\left(Re_\lambda/\sqrt{15}\right)^{1/2}\) (the isotropic relation \(\varepsilon=15\nu u'^2/\lambda^2\)). All radii are in units of \(\eta\), all velocities in units of \(u_\eta\) (written \(U_\eta\) in the model), and \(\Gamma\) in units of \(\nu\).

A0b. Physical equivalents of the DNS rows

A DNS is solved in dimensionless variables, \(\tilde x=x/\ell_0\), \(\tilde u=u/u_0\), \(\tilde t=t\,u_0/\ell_0\), \(\tilde\nu=\nu/(u_0\ell_0)\), \(\tilde\varepsilon=\varepsilon\,\ell_0/u_0^3\). Ghira et al. fix the box at \(2\pi\times2\pi\times2\pi\) (so \(\ell_0=L_{\text{box}}/2\pi\) and the Fourier wavenumbers are integers) and impose a random body force, delta-correlated in time and concentrated at the wavenumbers around \(k_p=2\) (Alvelius's scheme), whose mean power \(P=\langle f_iu_i\rangle=10\) is the same in every run. There are no walls and no mean flow; at steady state the viscous dissipation balances the power, so \(\tilde\varepsilon\approx10\) (Table II: 9.5–11.9) and the integral length \(\tilde L_{11}\approx0.85\) is set by the forcing. Only \(\tilde\nu\) changes between the runs (0.0145 to 0.0010), and it sets \(Re_\lambda\); the Kolmogorov length \(\tilde\eta=(\tilde\nu^3/\tilde\varepsilon)^{1/4}\) is the value listed in Table II (0.0235 for the first run). One series is therefore the same stirrer in fluids of falling viscosity.

To compare with a physical flow with kinematic viscosity \(\nu\) and dissipation rate \(\varepsilon\), the two scales \(\ell_0\) and \(u_0\) follow from two equations, \(\varepsilon=\tilde\varepsilon\,u_0^3/\ell_0\) and \(\nu=\tilde\nu\,u_0\ell_0\):

$$\ell_0=\frac{\eta_{\text{phys}}}{\tilde\eta}=\left(\frac{\nu^3/\varepsilon}{\tilde\nu^3/\tilde\varepsilon}\right)^{1/4},\qquad u_0=\frac{\nu}{\tilde\nu\,\ell_0},\qquad \eta_{\text{phys}}=\left(\frac{\nu^3}{\varepsilon}\right)^{1/4}.$$

The Reynolds number \(Re_\lambda\) is already used (through \(\tilde\nu\)), so one of \(\varepsilon\), \(\ell_0\) or \(u_0\) must be given besides \(\nu\). The model takes \(\varepsilon\) as given, as in section 5, and the reference used here is water, \(\nu=10^{-6}\) m\(^2\)/s, at \(\varepsilon=0.1\) W/kg, so \(\eta_{\text{phys}}=56.2\ \mu\)m. The results of the DNS in Kolmogorov units hold for any \(\varepsilon\); a different reference only rescales the lengths, and an \(r_{\max}\) of \(\beta\,\eta\) becomes \(\beta\times56.2\ \mu\)m. The chord correction (hot wire, all tubes)

A single wire or crossed wire records one velocity component along a straight path through the flow. With Taylor's hypothesis the time is a distance \(s=Ut\), and a tube is a vortex with Gaussian vorticity \(\omega=\omega_0e^{-\rho^2/2R^2}\) (\(R\) its Gaussian width, \(R_B=\sqrt2\,R\), \(r_{\max}=\sqrt{2x}\,R=1.585\,R\)) and azimuthal velocity \(v_\theta=\omega_0R^2(1-e^{-\rho^2/2R^2})/\rho\). For a tube with axis \(\mathbf a\) crossed by a path with direction \(\mathbf e_x\) at impact parameter \(b\) (the distance between the path and the axis), the distance of the path from the axis is \(\rho^2=b^2+\sin^2\gamma\,(s-s_c)^2\), with \(\gamma\) the angle between path and axis, and the signal in the sensed direction \(\mathbf e_y\) is

$$S(s)=\frac{v_\theta(\rho)}{\rho}\;\mathbf e_y\cdot\left(\mathbf a\times\mathbf p(s)\right).$$

For a tube normal to the path and crossed through its axis the signal is \(v_\theta(|s|)\,\mathrm{sgn}\,s\): two extrema at \(\pm r_{\max}\), a spacing of \(2r_{\max}=3.17R\) (the rule \(R=0.31\,U\Delta t\) of Belin et al.) and an amplitude \(U_{\max}\). A Burgers tube crossed by a straight path (A0c) gives a mean chord ratio of about 1.7 (tilt limit 45°, signal cut at 25–30% of the central amplitude), which brings the radius to \(5.1\,\eta\), the DNS value; the same crossings lower the amplitude by a factor 1.8 on average, and the all-tube average also contains weaker tubes (the authors attribute most of the low \(V_0\) to the true mean circulation velocity). The factor 3 is a working value, not derived; with it the swirl lies within 3% of the common curve. See A0c.
