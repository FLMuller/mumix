Remarks in the page: The method measures a time record, which the mean velocity \(U\) turns into a distance (frozen flow); the radius follows from the spacing of the two extrema of the signal as the filament passes, and the peak amplitude gives \(U_{\max}\). The value \(R=3.5\,\eta\) is the position of the maximum of the size distribution seen by the probe (Fig. 5, which collapses for \(Re_\lambda=151\)–718), quoted by the authors as the mean worm size; the same distribution built from the DNS of Jiménez et al. through the same probe geometry (their Eq. 9) peaks at 2.8 \(\eta\), so the probe sizes are 1.25 times the DNS sizes. The distribution is skewed with a tail to about 20 \(\eta\), and a rough reading of the figure puts its mean near 6 \(\eta\) and its median near 5 \(\eta\). The row uses the peak, \(r_{\max}=1.585\times3.5=5.5\,\eta\), as a range of \(Re_\lambda\). Above \(Re_\lambda\approx700\) the size distribution widens: the mean velocity increment across the filament decreases and its mean radius increases so that the circulation does not change; the authors read this as an instability of the filaments. The paper has no table of \(R\), \(U_{\max}\) or the circulation by \(Re_\lambda\).
