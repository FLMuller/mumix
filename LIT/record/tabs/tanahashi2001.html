7. Digitising the pdf
31
The pdf is drawn with a logarithmic vertical axis, and the curves of the figure are a band (several curves). The digitiser finds, at each horizontal position, the upper bound and the lower bound of the plotted curves, and takes \(P=(P_{top}+P_{bottom})/2\). The pixel rows are turned into values with the tick marks of the axis: \(\log_{10}P = \text{top exponent} - \dfrac{y-y_0}{y_1-y_0}\times\text{decades}\) (a straight line on the logarithmic axis, fixed by two labelled ticks).
32
Rules of the digitiser: the points where the lower bound is below 2×10⁻⁴ are dropped (those are the noisy single-count tail lines of the figure); the curve starts at the first point above that and ends where the band falls to the floor of the plot.

8. The radius, and the fit with two lognormals
37
The radius of maximum swirl is half the diameter: \(r_{\max}=D/2\), so \(p_r(r)=2\,p_D(2r)\). The curve is scaled to a total area of 1 (the digitised area, the rise below the first point and the tail beyond the last point are all counted: summary_pdfs.normalise). A single lognormal does not fit (the curve has a narrow core and a long tail), so two are fitted in the linear domain (a fit in log P weights the tail as much as the core, and the core is what we need), with the weight free, the medians ordered \(\mu_2>\mu_1\) and \(s_2\ge2s_1\) as you asked.
