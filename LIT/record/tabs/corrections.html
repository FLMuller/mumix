2. The hot wire reads a chord: the model behind the Mouri and Hori corrections
5
A hot wire records the velocity along a straight line through the flow (frozen turbulence). A vortex tube is rarely crossed through its axis at a right angle: the path passes at a distance \(b\) from the axis (the impact parameter) and the axis is tilted by an angle \(\alpha\). The record then shows a wider and weaker swirl than the tube has. chord_model.py computes what a straight path reads from a Burgers tube for a random offset and orientation, and which events survive an amplitude cut \(f\) (an event is kept when its peak is at least \(f\) times the peak of a central, normal crossing).

8
chord_model.py: the distribution of the measured radius over the true radius for random crossings, for several tilt limits and amplitude cuts.

3. The test of the chord model: the size distribution of Belin et al. (1996)
10
Belin et al. measured the distribution of the size of single events in helium (\(R_\lambda\) = 151–718). Their Fig. 5 was digitised (digitise_belin.py) and fitted (fit_chord.py) with: a lognormal distribution of true radii, seen through the chord distribution of the model. The broad measured distribution is reproduced by a narrow distribution of true radii crossed at random: this is the evidence that a chord distribution, not a wide range of tube sizes, makes most of the width seen by a probe.

4. The radius of Mouri and Hori (2009): ÷ 1.75
16
Mouri and Hori average the profile of all events and fit a Burgers profile to the mean: \(R_0=8.3\)–\(9.2\,\eta\) (mean 8.8). The chord model gives the ratio of such an all-event radius to the true radius as 1.7–1.85 (tilt limit 20–70°, true radius about 5). 8.8/1.75 = 5.0, the radius of the other sources. Rationale: the chord model and the agreement with the DNS radii.
17
fit_chordcorr.py: the fit with the hot-wire radii corrected.
18
The radius pdfs of all tubes (tabs of Tanahashi et al. and Pirozzoli) are each the sum of two lognormal functions. The first is narrow (width 0.29–0.35) and holds 53–71% of the tubes: median 5.2, 4.9, 4.3 \(\eta\) and mean 5.5, 5.3, 4.6 \(\eta\) (Tanahashi 2001, Tanahashi 2004, Pirozzoli 2012). This first function is the \(r_{\max}/\eta\) of the model, 5. The second is broad (median 8.5–16.8 \(\eta\)) and raises the mean of the whole pdf to 7.2–8.2.

23
Review. (i) fit_threshold.py itself found that the chord model explains the radius of the all-tube average but not its swirl: the swirl needs a factor 4.5–5.9 that grows with \(Re_\lambda\), outside the range of the model; fit_smoothing.py added the length of the wire to close the gap. (ii) Mouri and Hori state in their abstract that the swirl of all tubes scales with \(u_K\) 'in contrast to the case of intense vortex tubes alone where the circulation velocity scales with the rms velocity fluctuation'. (iii) Fitting the all-tube sources alone (Kida et al. and Mouri and Hori with a free factor; modelselect.py) gives a factor 1.84 and \(U_{\max}/U_\eta=2.0\,Re_\lambda^{0.13}\) with an rms of 1.3%; a factor 3 gives 3.9% and an exponent 0.33 that neither source shows on its own (0.15 within Mouri and Hori, 0.09 within Kida et al.).
