2. The selection of the model (model I: one population)
5
Every candidate is fitted to the same data by minimising the weighted sum of squares S of the last appendix of the paper (relative plus absolute error, linear domain; s = sqrt(S/(N-p)) is quoted in per cent). Three criteria: AICc and BIC (information criteria; a difference under 2 is no evidence, over 10 is decisive), counted with one unit a source-quantity group, and LOSO (leave one source out: the model is fitted without a source and predicts it). LOSO is the criterion used; among the best the model with the fewest parameters is taken.

4. Model II: two populations, no swirl correction
12
The swirl as calculated from the papers splits into two families. Intense tubes (the 1% of highest vorticity in DNS: Ghira et al.; the 0.1% threshold of the hot wire: Mouri et al. 2007): \(U_{\max}/U_\eta=1.83\,Re_\lambda^{0.26}\), rms 12%, 20 points, no correction. All tubes (Kida et al.; Mouri and Hori with one fitted chord factor): \(U_{\max}/U_\eta=1.73\,Re_\lambda^{0.16}\) with a chord factor 1.98, rms 5.5%. The exponent inside Mouri and Hori alone is 0.16 and inside Kida et al. alone 0.09: the all-tube swirl grows slowly, as Mouri and Hori say (it scales with \(u_K\)).

Kida et al. also report that the vortex cores hold 31% of the dissipation. A Burgers vortex has only 10% of its swirl dissipation inside \(r_{\max}\) (37% inside \(1.5\,r_{\max}\), 61% inside \(2\,r_{\max}\)): most of the dissipation of a tube is in the annulus around the core, as Kida et al. describe. The model's vortex share of the dissipation (88% at \(\beta=5\)) is the whole swirl field, so the two numbers are not in conflict, but they are not a quantitative check either.
