2. The selection of the model (model I: one population, the corrections as decided)
5
Every candidate is fitted to the same data by least squares of the log residuals. Three criteria: AICc and BIC (information criteria; a difference under 2 is no evidence, over 10 is decisive), counted with one unit a source-quantity group, and LOSO: each source in turn is left out, the model fitted to the rest predicts it, and the rms of those errors is the score. LOSO penalises a parameter that follows the noise of one source, so the penalty on complexity comes from the data. (The earlier rule was: the fewest parameters within 25% of the best rms.)

Reading. L1 (radius 5, swirl \(a+b\ln Re_\lambda\), two parameters) predicts left-out sources best (9.0%); D6 (radius 5, swirl \(c\,Re_\lambda^{1/3}\), one parameter) follows at 10.4%, and the information criteria cannot separate them (ΔAICc 0.7). No model with three or four parameters does better out of sample than these two: the extra parameters fit noise. With the penalty on complexity the one-parameter model stays: \(U_{\max}/U_\eta=0.769\,Re_\lambda^{1/3}\), \(r_{\max}=5\,\eta\). L1 is \(U_{\max}/U_\eta=-4.61+1.80\ln Re_\lambda\); it goes negative below \(Re_\lambda\approx13\), which a power law cannot.

4. Model II: two populations, no swirl correction
12
The swirl as calculated from the papers splits into two families. Intense tubes (the 1% of highest vorticity in DNS: Ghira et al.; the 0.1% threshold of the hot wire: Mouri et al. 2007): \(U_{\max}/U_\eta=1.29\,Re_\lambda^{0.32}\), rms 12%, 20 points, no correction. All tubes (Kida et al.; Mouri and Hori with one fitted chord factor): \(U_{\max}/U_\eta=2.00\,Re_\lambda^{0.13}\) with a chord factor 1.84, rms 1.3%. The exponent inside Mouri and Hori alone is 0.15 and inside Kida et al. alone 0.09: the all-tube swirl grows slowly, as Mouri and Hori say (it scales with \(u_K\)).

Kida et al. also report that the vortex cores hold 31% of the dissipation. A Burgers vortex has only 10% of its swirl dissipation inside \(r_{\max}\) (37% inside \(1.5\,r_{\max}\), 61% inside \(2\,r_{\max}\)): most of the dissipation of a tube is in the annulus around the core, as Kida et al. describe. The model's vortex share of the dissipation (88% at \(\beta=5\)) is the whole swirl field, so the two numbers are not in conflict, but they are not a quantitative check either.
